Injection sites
Where to shoot · L and R are yours, not the diagram's
RecommendedUsableUsed recentlyAvoid
  • Abdomen is first choice. Most subcutaneous fat, most consistent absorption, easiest to reach. Stay outside a two inch radius from the navel, the dashed circle.
  • Flanks are a close second. The love handle area holds fat reliably and is very forgiving.
  • Thighs and upper outer glute are solid backups. Use the outer third of the thigh, not the front or inner surface. On the glute, upper outer quadrant only.
  • Back of the arm works but is awkward solo. Hard to pinch one-handed. Fine if someone else is doing it.
  • Rotate every time. Sites marked with a slash were used in the last five days. Repeated use in one spot builds scar tissue and absorption gets unpredictable.
  • Six injections a day saturates this map. Fourteen sites against six daily doses means every spot gets used roughly every other day, and there is no rotation pattern that avoids it. The app picks the site that has rested longest, but if you start seeing persistent lumps or redness, that is the schedule talking, not your technique.
  • Pinch, then go in at 45 to 90 degrees. Pinch an inch of skin, insert, release the pinch, inject slowly, count to five before withdrawing.
  • Skip any spot that is bruised, red, tender, scarred, or has a mole or stretch mark. Also avoid the waistband line where clothing rubs all day.
  • GHK-Cu wants the fattiest site you have. It stings more than the others, and thin skin makes it worse. Abdomen or flank, and never the same spot twice in a week.
  • BPC-157 can go near the injury if you are treating something specific and the anatomy allows a safe subcutaneous pinch there.
  • NAD+ needs room. It is a 25 unit volume, so use abdomen, glute, or thigh rather than a thin site, and push slowly.
